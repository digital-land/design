The Use Classes Order is a deregulatory tool: grouping together uses into classes and providing that movement between such uses is not development. In order to provide maximum flexibility in support of high streets and town centres, and in common with other use classes, the Commercial, Business and Service use class is not limited in respect of size or location etc, and premises can move freely between uses within that class. The National Planning Policy Framework requires local planning authorities to apply a sequential test to planning applications for main town centre uses which are neither in an existing centre nor in accordance with an up-to-date plan. However, movement within the Commercial, Business and Service use class does not require an application for planning permission and therefore there is no opportunity to apply a sequential test. To do so would add planning process where currently there is none, and reduce the flexibility currently afforded on the high street. The recent consultation on reforms to national planning policy noted that we propose, as part of a broader update, to review the approach to town centre and out-of-centre development in the light of the use class changes.
